8C. Worked Bayesian decision-making example (expected-cost minimization)
==============================================================================
Example student (test-set index 187): posterior P(probation) = 0.502
Cost matrix used (illustrative, set by the consultancy in consultation with the department — not derived from data):
  Intervene & student was NOT at risk: cost = 1 (one unnecessary advising slot)
  Intervene & student WAS at risk:     cost = 0 (correct catch)
  No action & student was NOT at risk: cost = 0 (correct)
  No action & student WAS at risk:     cost = 5 (missed at-risk student — costliest)

E[cost | intervene]  = 0.502*0 + 0.498*1 = 0.498
E[cost | no action]  = 0.502*5 + 0.498*0 = 2.508
Decision (minimize expected cost): INTERVENE (flag for advising)

How the optimal flagging threshold shifts with the assumed cost ratio (missing an at-risk student vs. one false alarm):
 cost_ratio_miss_vs_false_alarm  break_even_threshold
                              1                 0.500
                              2                 0.333
                              5                 0.167
                             10                 0.091
Note: a plain 0.5 cutoff (implicitly equal costs) is a special/default case, not a principled choice — this is precisely what Bayesian decision theory makes explicit and adjustable, unlike a bare classification-accuracy read of Task 5's confusion matrices.
